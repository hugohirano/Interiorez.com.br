An image on a screen shows how a room could look. A sample in your hand shows how it will be. Before committing to a renovation, placing the materials side by side is one of the simplest ways to avoid expensive regrets.

## Why the physical sample matters

Screens change colour and gloss. Catalogue photos are taken under controlled light. Only the real piece shows the texture, the visual weight and how a finish responds to touch and use.

With natural stone, the difference is even greater. Every slab has its own pattern, and a small sample does not always represent the whole. If the stone is the main feature, it is worth seeing the full slab before you buy.

## What to put in your sample set

Gather everything that will cover a visible area:

- floor and skirting;
- stone for worktops and cladding;
- timbers and laminates for joinery;
- paints and wall textures;
- fabrics for curtains and upholstery;
- metals, such as handles and taps.

Keep proportion in mind. A material covering the whole floor carries far more weight in the composition than a handle, even when the samples are the same size.

## How to assess the samples

**See them in the right place.** Put each piece where it will be installed, in its real position. A floor sample should lie flat and a wall sample should stand upright.

**Look at different times of day.** Morning light, late afternoon light and artificial lighting can change the same colour considerably.

**Test for use.** Ask how the material responds to stains, scratches and moisture, and what maintenance it needs. The most beautiful choice also has to fit daily life.

**Compare everything together.** Two materials can be excellent apart and compete when placed side by side. It generally works well to let one lead and have the others support it.

## Keep the references

Note the manufacturer, name, code and batch of each item. Ceramics and stone can vary in tone between batches, and that information helps a great deal if material runs out during the works.

Once the sample set is settled, the next decisions become easier, because each new choice already has something to respond to.
